### F372 — F371's clamp used the wrong signal (`employee.start_date`, not the entity-scoping/calendar edges that actually determine an answer); corrected to a null-returning variant of G08's own resolution, shared with no duplication

**Found while:** Codex's UI-3 implementation, applying F371's ruled clamp. Two residual failures surfaced and were reported together: (1) the browser fixture's employees have `start_date` 60 days in the past but were scoped to their (freshly created) Entity only today — F371's clamp to `employee.start_date` still reaches lead-in dates with no `scoped_to_entity` edge or calendar in force, so the aggregate call still 500s; (2) `getBenchForecastCosts`'s annual-frequency cost denominator calls `countWorkingDaysFromGraph` from each `${year}-01-01`, entirely outside F371's three guarded loops, and hits the identical unguarded `resolvedDayOn` failure whenever an Entity's calendar postdates January 1st of the queried year. Independently verified against the fixture (`packages/graph/sync-browser-tests/bench-forecast.spec.ts`'s `seed()`: `employee.create` with `effective_from: <today>` but `fields.start_date: shift(today, -60)`), `services/api/src/graph/working-days.ts` (`resolvedDayOn`, `countWorkingDays`), `entity-resolution.ts::resolveEntityAssignment`, and `services/api/src/permission/bench-forecast-queries.ts`'s four call sites (the three F371 touched, plus the annual-denominator loop at line ~479).

**Why F371's clamp was the wrong fix, not an incomplete one.** `employee.start_date` is a general HR fact — when someone joined the firm — not the fact that determines whether `resolvedDayOn` can answer: that fact is whether a `scoped_to_entity` edge (and a resolvable calendar for that entity) was in force on the queried date, which `resolveEntityAssignment`/`resolveCalendarForEntity` already compute precisely, returning `null` when it wasn't. The fixture's scenario — a long-tenured employee moved into a newly created Entity — is not a fixture artifact; it is exactly how an entity restructuring or new-subsidiary formation would look in real data, and F371's clamp does not cover it because `start_date` and "date first scoped to *this* entity" are genuinely different facts that can diverge by any amount. Comparable date-string arithmetic at each call site cannot be made correct by adding more fields to compare against, because the caller does not (and should not) reimplement `resolveEntityAssignment`'s and `resolveCalendarForEntity`'s own resolution logic to compute the right bound in advance — that logic already exists, once, in `working-days.ts`/`entity-resolution.ts`/`calendar-resolution.ts` per G08's own stated principle of a single implementation.

**Corrected fix: ask the resolver directly, don't pre-guess its answer.** Extract `resolvedDayOn`'s existing body into a new function `resolvedDayOnOrNull` in `services/api/src/graph/working-days.ts`, changing only its two structural-absence branches (`if (!assignment) ...`, `if (!calendar) ...`) from `throw new Error("not-found")` to `return null`; every other branch — the employee-not-found check, and every `mayRead` permission check (employee, assignment's entity, calendar) — keeps throwing exactly as today, so a genuine permission denial is never silently absorbed. Redefine `resolvedDayOn` itself as a two-line wrapper: `const day = await resolvedDayOnOrNull(...); if (day === null) throw new Error("not-found"); return day;`. This is a zero-behavior-change refactor for every existing caller of `resolvedDayOn` — `mutations/assignment.ts`, `mutations/timesheet-anomaly.ts`, `router.ts`'s exposed `countWorkingDays` procedure, `permission/leave-queries.ts`, and `bench-forecast-queries.ts`'s own callers all keep throwing under exactly the same conditions they do today, since `resolvedDayOn`'s throws are provably identical before and after. Only Bench Forecast's own four date-range loops (`benchDaysForWindow`, `utilizationTotals`, `getBenchForecastCosts`'s per-region loop, and its annual-denominator loop) switch to calling `resolvedDayOnOrNull` directly and treat a `null` result as "not a working day, contributes nothing" — skip the date, exactly as they already skip a date covered by an assignment or excluded as a Pitch day. `countWorkingDays` itself (the shared G08 utility used by `router.ts`'s own exposed procedure and `leave-queries.ts`'s TOIL/leave-year math) is not touched and keeps its strict, whole-range throw — those consumers may legitimately want a calendar to exist for every day of a queried range, and this ruling does not decide that question for them. `getBenchForecastCosts`'s annual-denominator computation stops calling the shared `countWorkingDaysFromGraph` and instead loops its own year range with `resolvedDayOnOrNull`, counting only the days that resolve, so an Entity created partway through a year correctly divides by the working days that existed, not by an error.

F371's three `employee.record["start_date"]` comparisons in `benchDaysForWindow`, `utilizationTotals`, and `getBenchForecastCosts`'s per-region loop are removed — they are superseded, not supplemented, by this more precise check, which is strictly more correct (it also excludes a date after `start_date` but before the entity-scoping/calendar edge existed, the exact case that broke F371's version) and needs nothing to be true about the relationship between `start_date` and entity-scoping dates.

**Ruling. Closed 29 September 2026, corrected mechanical extension of G08's own existing resolution logic (zero-behavior-change refactor plus four call-site changes, all inside `bench-forecast-queries.ts`), no founder ruling needed.** This narrows, again, only the caller-side handling of a `null` structural-absence result Bench Forecast's own functions already receive from primitives that have not changed; it does not add, remove, or reorder any permission check, and every non-Bench-Forecast caller of `resolvedDayOn`/`countWorkingDays` is provably unaffected.

**Status: closed** (corrected mechanical fix, folded into the corrected UI-3 brief/implementation, supersedes F371's specific clamp mechanism without reopening F371's underlying ruling that pre-existence lead-in days must not error).
