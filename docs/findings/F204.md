### F204 — a `User` node has no workspace, but `graph_nodes` requires one

Raised 21 September 2026, at the start of Stage 2 of the server-authoritative build.

**The conflict.** `VPS-A002`'s Universal node conventions give every node a `workspace_id` except two: the Workspace itself, and the `User`, which "carries none — a person may hold WorkspaceMembership in more than one workspace; scoping and role live on the membership, not the identity". The Stage 2 brief makes `graph_nodes.workspace_id` `not null` and `node_id` the primary key, and says the Workspace node's `workspace_id` equals its own `node_id`. The Workspace case is solved by that rule. The `User` case is not: a person who creates or joins a second workspace has one `User` node and no single workspace to put in the column.

**Why this was not decided in code.** Every candidate changes the schema or the sync design: a null `workspace_id` for `User` rows, a separate row per workspace under a different `node_id`, or keeping `User` out of `graph_nodes` (which breaks the `membership_of` edge's foreign key to it). The columns, indexes and the shape filter in Stage 6 all follow from the choice.

**Closed by founder-delegated decision, 21 September 2026.** The recommendation above — a null `workspace_id` for `User` rows — was rejected, because a row that belongs to no workspace breaks per-workspace residency (`VPS-A008` P8), export and erasure, and would let one workspace's edits to a person's node appear in another's. Instead a `User` is stored once per workspace it belongs to, under the same `node_id` (its Better Auth account id). `graph_nodes` has the composite primary key `(workspace_id, node_id)`, every other node type's `node_id` stays globally unique through a partial index, and edges reference their endpoints by `(workspace_id, node_id)`, so an edge can never join two workspaces. `VPS-A002` and `VPS-A003` were amended on `main`. Built in Stage 2.
