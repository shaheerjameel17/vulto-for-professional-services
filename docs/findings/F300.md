### F300 — the spec's interceptor-filtering language and command-availability implication have no local mechanism

Two remaining claims in `VPS-F002`. (a) Its Permission-filtering section says results "pass through `VPS-A004`'s interceptor" and are "filtered again by role at query time", and G03 says results are "filtered through `VPS-A004`'s interceptor, identical to every other read". The interceptor runs on the server; a query over the device's own local index cannot call it, and does not need to. What the device holds is already the person's own audience, derived from `decideRead`'s outcome by `audience-recompute` (including F292's `readScope` widening for `Employee:operational`), so a purely local read over the cache returns exactly what the person may read — the identical reasoning Stage 22's `skillMatrix.get` G03 ruling recorded and its completion review confirmed against `bench-forecast.ts`'s own precedent. (b) The Commands group implies that "create employee" and "add assignment" appear only to a caller who may do those things, but the spec never states that any command is hidden from any role, and a role signal, while it does exist on the device (F261: the replicated `WorkspaceMembership` reached through `membership_of` and resolved by `deriveEffectiveRoles`, which `conflictCheck.evaluate` already uses for its Owner check), has nothing to be evaluated against for a create-style action — `employee.create` and `assignment.create` have no existing row, so a row-scoped policy cell such as Manager's `Full (direct reports)` on `Employee:operational` cannot be resolved locally, and no precedent exists for deciding a create locally. `VPS-F002` itself states "this feature constructs no permission logic of its own".

**Closed by direct precedent, no founder consultation needed.** For (a), Stage 22's own G03 ruling: no second authorization check is reimplemented client-side, and the spec's Permission-filtering section and G03 are corrected to say the device cache's audience is the filter for the local half, while the server-backed groups run through the interceptor. The empty-state rule is unaffected — locally there is nothing filtered to distinguish from nothing matched, since a restricted row is simply not on the device. For (b), commands are a static typed registry naming a symbolic destination or action, matched by text only, with no role gating in this stage; every executed command is authorized by the server on the mutation or read it triggers, as every write already is, and a permission refusal reaches the person through the shell's ordinary rejection surface. This matches the shell prototype's own comment ("a rendering capability, not an authorization decision — every action…", `apps/roster-web/src/app/devices/devices-client.tsx`) and is recorded as a known limitation: role-aware command visibility is a UI-stage concern, feasible later on the existing local role derivation once a decision exists for how a create is evaluated without a row, and not something to invent in this stage. Command execution itself (`VPS-F002-S05`) belongs to the shell, the same deferral every earlier Interface Specification has received.

**Status: closed.**
