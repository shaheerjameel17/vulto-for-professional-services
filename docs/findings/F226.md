### F226 — the working-day index's materialization is a later stage's problem, not this one's

Raised 22 September 2026 while briefing Stage 12, tracing `VRS-F004`'s Technical Architecture before writing the brief. The spec's "Materialization, and why it matters" section states the working-day index is "materialized in the SQLite-WASM index," rebuilt "in the worker," sized entirely against `VRS-F005`'s Bench Forecast rendering "150 employees across 90 days within 200ms" — a feature not yet built and with no scheduled stage. The three Non-Functional Requirements this drives (5ms/1ms read budgets, a 500ms incremental-rebuild budget "in the worker") are all specific to that index. Checked directly: nothing in `packages/graph/src/mutators/foundation.ts` or `packages/graph/src/sync-client` currently implements any per-employee-per-date materialized table, and no caller anywhere in the current codebase would exercise one at the volume that motivates it. The spec's own language — "materialized in the SQLite-WASM index," "in the worker" — describes the CRDT-era client-side materialization pipeline `VPS-A001` retired with the September 2026 server-authoritative pivot (Loro and its Worker-private materialization are gone; `wa-sqlite` is retained, but now "fed by replication rather than by CRDT materialization," per `VPS-A001`'s own corrected text). Building the index as originally specified, before its one consumer exists and before its own supporting architecture has been re-examined under the new model, risks building the wrong thing twice.

**Ruling — closed 22 September 2026, mirroring precedent already set twice in this project.** Stage 11's own brief deferred `entity.resolveForEmployee`'s real caller ("this stage has no real caller yet... prove it with tests") rather than build speculative consumers; Stage 8 and Stage 11 both deferred `apps/` UI work with no scheduled stage. The same judgment applies here, at larger scale: Stage 12 builds the complete, correct `WorkingCalendar`/`Holiday`/`WorkingPattern` data model and the full `workingDays.*` public API (G01–G05, G08), each function evaluating the spec's own resolution order directly against the graph on every call — no cache, no precomputed table, no index. This is correct, testable, and sufficient for everything Stage 12 itself needs to prove, including every one of the spec's acceptance criteria that does not depend on the index's specific performance numbers. G06 (the index's existence and materialization) and the index-rebuild half of G07 are deferred in full to whichever stage builds `VRS-F005` — the feature whose render budget is the only reason the index exists — where it will be designed against the server-authoritative architecture as it stands at that time, not against the retired one this document's prose still describes. G07's three recalculation exemptions (a closed PayRun, an approved LeaveRequest, a signed Contract) are **not** deferred: they are business rules about what recalculates, independent of whether recalculation is driven by an index rebuild or a plain re-read, and Stage 12 proves them directly — a confirmed holiday's shifted date is simply visible on `workingDays.*`'s very next call, with the same three record types correctly excluded. `VRS-F004`'s G06, G07, its Materialization section, its Non-Functional Requirements and its Decisions Recorded section are all corrected to record this staging split explicitly.

**Status: closed.** Stage 12 is unblocked; it builds the full feature except the index, which remains an explicit, tracked deferral rather than a silent gap. Whichever issue builds `VRS-F005` inherits the obligation to design and build G06/G07's index at that time.
